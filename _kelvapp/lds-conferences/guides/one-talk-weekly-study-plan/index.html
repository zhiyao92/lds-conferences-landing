A simple one-talk-a-week General Conference study plan

Build a manageable General Conference study routine with one address, a few useful notes and a thoughtful return later in the week.

By Kelvapp · 7 November 2026 · 3 min read

After General Conference, it is easy to plan an ambitious reading routine. Every address. Detailed notes. Perhaps a color system so elaborate that it deserves its own training session. Then an ordinary week arrives.

A one-talk-a-week plan gives you a smaller place to begin. You can use LDS Conferences to find an address, adjust the reading layout, keep highlights and notes, and return to a favorite. The schedule below is a suggested personal routine, not an official study requirement.

Choose one address for a reason

Start with a talk you want to understand better, not the one you think you ought to finish first. Perhaps you remember the speaker, have a question from a recent conversation or want to revisit something you heard but did not fully absorb.

LDS Conferences lets you browse by conference, session or speaker and search by talk title or speaker. If you only remember a topic or a few words, the official General Conference library is another place to look. Once you identify the address, keep its title and speaker so you can find it again.

First session: read for the overall message

Give yourself one comfortable reading session early in the week. Adjust text size, typeface, spacing or the reader’s appearance before you begin. There is no prize for squinting at a sentence you intend to ponder.

Read to understand the whole address before deciding which lines belong in your notes. If you prefer to watch, the app offers an address’s original video when available. Media availability depends on the source, and streamed content needs a connection. Check before planning to study somewhere without internet access.

Keep fewer highlights, with better notes

Highlight the passages you genuinely want to revisit. If you highlight nearly everything, the next session becomes a rereading of a brightly colored talk rather than a useful return to your questions.

Alongside a selected passage, add a personal note that explains why it matters. Try one of these prompts: “What am I being asked to consider?” “What context do I need?” or “What question would I like to study next?” Separate your interpretation from the speaker’s words.

Midweek: revisit the context

Return to the saved address for a shorter session. Read the paragraphs around your highlighted passage, not just the isolated line. Check any relevant source references before building a conclusion around a quotation.

You may understand the passage differently after a few days. Edit your personal note if needed. A study record is allowed to show changing understanding; it does not have to look as though you had a polished answer immediately.

Choose one modest response

If you want to act on something you read, make the response specific and realistic. You could set aside time for another source, prepare a thoughtful question or make space for a conversation you have been postponing.

Avoid turning someone else into your study project. If your response involves another person, consider what would actually be welcome. Your notes can guide your own reflection without becoming an unsolicited lesson for the next person you meet.

End the week without creating a backlog

At the end of the week, write one sentence about what you want to remember. Save the talk as a favorite if you expect to return. Reading history can also help you pick up where you left off.

If you missed a session, continue when you can. You do not need to double next week’s workload. The app also offers reading challenges if you prefer a guided structure, but choose a pace that remains useful rather than treating a streak as the purpose.

LDS Conferences is independently published. Use official Church sources for authoritative guidance and current announcements. For an approachable start, find one address in LDS Conferences and give yourself a week to return to it with attention.
